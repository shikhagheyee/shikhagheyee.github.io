How I Work

The Four Pillars of My Documentation Practice

Principles developed across enterprise migrations, API platforms, and AI knowledge bases.

01

Structured Information Architecture

Users do not read software manuals cover-to-cover; they scan for answers. I structure content into modular, task-oriented topics using DITA and component-based authoring, ensuring every article has clear prerequisites, action steps, and outcomes.

02

Microcopy & UX Writing Governance

Good documentation begins directly in the product interface. By auditing and standardizing over 18,000 UI text strings, I ensure that button labels, modals, error messages, and tooltips guide users seamlessly before they ever need a help doc.

03

AI-Native Content Systems

Modern documentation must serve two audiences: human users and AI agents. I craft semantic taxonomies and prompt frameworks that keep release notes consistent while structuring knowledge chunks for LLM assistants to retrieve accurately.

04

Editorial Standards & Enablement

Documentation is an organizational muscle. I institute style guides, establish peer-review workflows, and mentor junior writers so product and engineering teams can produce consistent, high-quality technical content at scale.

UI & Microcopy Standardization Darwinbox

Overhauling 18,000+ UI Text Strings Across Core Enterprise Modules

Partnered with Product and UX design teams to audit, rewrite, and govern more than 18,000 user interface strings across mission-critical human resource and payroll workflows.

Before (Developer Jargon)
"Error 5042: Transaction rolled back. Unable to commit batch records to database due to invalid key constraints."

Ambiguous technical failure that caused panic and forced users to raise high-priority IT tickets.

After (Clear, Human, Actionable)
"Unable to finalize this payroll batch. Review 3 employees with missing tax registration IDs to complete processing."

Specific, calm, and actionable with a direct link to the affected records, cutting support volume.
